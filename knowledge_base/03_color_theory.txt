COLOR THEORY IN FASHION: THE COMPLETE GUIDE
Source: glance.com, cedarandlilyclothier.com

==================================================
FOUNDATIONS OF COLOR THEORY
==================================================

THREE PILLARS:
- HUE: The name and classification of a color (red, blue, green, purple)
- VALUE: How light or dark a color is (baby pink vs. deep maroon, sky blue vs. navy)
- INTENSITY: How bright or muted a color is (neon orange vs. rust orange)

WARM vs. COOL TONES:
- Warm tones (red, yellow, orange, gold) convey energy, passion, and approachability. They suit festive, ethnic, and casual occasions.
- Cool tones (blue, green, purple, silver) suggest calmness, professionalism, and elegance. They work well in corporate settings and formal wear.
- Neutral tones (black, white, grey, beige, brown) are the backbone of any wardrobe — they complement both warm and cool accents.

==================================================
THE COLOR WHEEL — FIVE KEY SCHEMES
==================================================

1. MONOCHROMATIC:
Different shades, tints, and tones of the same color.
Example: Pale blue shirt + medium blue jeans + navy blazer
Effect: Polished, elongating, sophisticated
Best for: Professional settings, minimalist aesthetics

2. ANALOGOUS:
Colors that are adjacent on the color wheel.
Example: Yellow-orange top + orange accessories + red accents
Effect: Harmonious and cohesive without being flat
Best for: Casual and creative outfits, warm autumn palettes

3. COMPLEMENTARY:
Colors opposite each other on the color wheel.
Example: Navy blue top + orange bag/accessories
Effect: Bold, dynamic, eye-catching
Key rule: Let one color dominate (70%) and use the other as an accent (30%)

4. TRIADIC:
Three colors evenly spaced on the color wheel.
Example: Red, blue, and yellow in one outfit
Effect: Vibrant and playful
Best for: Casual and creative contexts; keep one color dominant

5. SPLIT-COMPLEMENTARY:
A base color plus the two colors on either side of its direct complement.
Example: Blue base + orange-red and yellow-orange accents
Effect: Visually interesting with less tension than full complementary

==================================================
SKIN TONE MATCHING
==================================================

HOW TO FIND YOUR UNDERTONE:
- Look at your wrist veins: greenish = warm undertone; bluish/purple = cool undertone; a mix = neutral
- Gold jewelry: if gold looks better on you = warm undertone
- Silver jewelry: if silver looks better on you = cool undertone

WARM UNDERTONES:
Best colors: Earthy tones (camel, terracotta, rust), warm neutrals (cream, ivory), peach, coral, mustard, olive green, warm reds (tomato, brick)
Avoid: Cool pastels, icy blues and pinks

COOL UNDERTONES:
Best colors: Jewel tones (emerald, sapphire, ruby, amethyst), icy pastels, true red, fuchsia, cool greys, burgundy, navy
Avoid: Orange-based tones, earthy yellows, warm browns

NEUTRAL UNDERTONES:
Most colors work. Muted, slightly desaturated versions of any hue look particularly elegant.

==================================================
OCCASION-BASED COLOR CHOICES
==================================================

PROFESSIONAL/INTERVIEWS: Navy, charcoal grey, black — convey confidence, authority, and competence
FIRST DATES: Red or burgundy — convey warmth and passion; dusty rose or blush for approachability
CASUAL / BRUNCH: Pastels, soft neutrals, warm whites — relaxed and inviting
FESTIVE / HOLIDAY: Jewel tones (emerald, ruby, sapphire), metallics (gold, silver), deep reds
BEACH / VACATION: Coral, turquoise, bright white, tropical prints
FORMAL EVENING: Black, midnight navy, deep jewel tones, classic red

COLOR PSYCHOLOGY:
- RED: Passion, energy, confidence — the most attention-commanding color
- BLUE: Calmness, trust, professionalism — the most universally liked color
- GREEN: Balance, growth, freshness — works in both casual and formal contexts
- YELLOW: Optimism, creativity, joy — best as an accent or in muted mustard/gold
- BLACK: Power, sophistication, versatility — the foundational wardrobe color
- WHITE: Purity, freshness, minimalism — clean and modern
- BURGUNDY: Elegance, depth, warmth — autumn/winter authority color

==================================================
PRACTICAL COLOR COMBINATION RULES
==================================================

ALWAYS-WORK PAIRINGS:
- White + navy (classic nautical, works in every context)
- Black + camel (sophisticated and modern)
- White + denim blue (effortless and fresh)
- Black + grey (timeless monochromatic)
- Beige + burgundy (festive elegance)
- Olive + mustard (earthy autumn harmony)
- Navy + white + tan (preppy but polished)
- Blush + grey (soft and feminine but modern)

THE 60-30-10 RULE:
- 60% dominant color (usually a neutral): the main pieces
- 30% secondary color: tops, jackets, bags
- 10% accent color: accessories, shoes, jewelry

TIPS FOR MIXING COLORS:
- Keep the big pieces (coat, trousers, skirt) in neutrals or muted tones
- Save saturated color for smaller pieces and accessories
- Use accessories to safely introduce a new color into your wardrobe
- Stick to 2-3 colors per outfit — more than that becomes chaotic

TIMELESS FOUNDATION COLORS FOR ANY WARDROBE
==================================================

Five colors that work universally and form the backbone of a versatile wardrobe:

1. BLACK — slimming, formal, pairs with everything
2. WHITE/OFF-WHITE — clean, modern, works across seasons
3. GREY — versatile, understated, office-to-casual
4. NAVY — the sophisticated alternative to black
5. CAMEL/TAN — warm neutral, elevated, perfect for outerwear

Build your core wardrobe in these five, then introduce season-specific accent colors.
